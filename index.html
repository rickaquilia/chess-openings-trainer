      The principles underneath all of it

      Every opening in this trainer is just these six ideas wearing different clothes. When a game leaves the lines you know, fall back on them.

      Fight for the center

      Pawns on e4/d4 (or e5/d5) control the squares your pieces want. Whoever owns the center usually owns the game.

      Develop with purpose

      Knights before bishops, one move per piece in the opening, and develop toward the center. A lead in development is a real advantage.

      Castle early

      Get the king safe and connect your rooks, ideally before move 10. Most opening disasters are uncastled kings caught in the center.

      Don't grab pawns early

      A pawn is rarely worth two tempi and open lines against your king. If a pawn looks free in the opening, ask what it costs to take it.

      Play where your pawns point

      A pawn chain aims at one side of the board. Attack on that side, and break the enemy chain at its base, not its head.

      Have a plan by move 12

      When the book moves end, don't drift. Pick the pawn break your structure calls for and aim every piece at making it happen.
